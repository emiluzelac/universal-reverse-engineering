# Hardware, electronics, and mechanical mechanisms

Use for consumer products, safe low-energy prototypes, photographed devices, machine designs, and mechanical systems.

## Safety gate

Start with the exterior, labels, public manuals, and user-provided measurements. **Do not suggest disassembly, probing, energizing, bypassing interlocks, or modifying** mains-powered, high-voltage, high-current, battery-pack, pressurized, combustible, medical, automotive safety, or industrial equipment. When hazard is uncertain, keep the investigation non-invasive and recommend a qualified technician.

## Method

1. Define the observed function, input energy/matter/information, and output.
2. Identify likely subsystems (sensor, actuator, control, power, frame, interface, thermal path, fluid path). Distinguish visible parts from suspected parts.
3. Draw a block diagram. Add likely mechanical/electrical constraints, tolerances, and operating conditions without inventing measurements.
4. Consider at least two mechanisms that could explain the observation.
5. Prefer safe external tests: compare documented operating modes, observe visible timing or motion, measure externally available non-hazardous dimensions, review teardown photos or diagrams the user has permission to use.
6. Compare with relevant physics: energy balance, force/torque, friction, heat transfer, sensor feedback, materials, latency, and failure modes.

## Report

Explain the energy and information flow, role of each visible component, competing internal mechanisms, safety limits, and what additional documentation or professional measurement would distinguish the hypotheses.

**Important:** An image can identify visible features; it cannot prove unseen internal circuitry, firmware, or materials.
