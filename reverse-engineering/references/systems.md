# Processes, products, and systems thinking

Use when reverse engineering an everyday process, company workflow, manufacturing concept, service, research apparatus, or unfamiliar technical idea without direct access to its internals.

## Procedure

1. Specify a measurable purpose: what goes in and what comes out, for whom, and under what constraints?
2. Draw a system boundary and list external actors, resources, information flows, and feedback.
3. Decompose into no more than seven major stages, then expand only the critical stage.
4. Identify bottlenecks, queues, irreversible actions, quality controls, decisions, exception paths, and potential incentives.
5. Locate observable artifacts: labels, public manuals, forms, reports, specifications, receipts, timestamps, diagrams, or user-provided samples.
6. Form competing explanations for unseen stages, then predict observable differences and propose low-risk ways to verify them.
7. Identify which conclusions represent **engineering possibility**, **common industry practice**, or **verified behavior of this exact target**. Never collapse these categories.

## Explain for learning

Offer a child-friendly analogy, an engineer's model, and a 'what I would look at next' section if the user is curious. For technical systems, include a simple ASCII flow diagram or state diagram.
